## PLATFORM TRUTH STATEMENT

Before any page is designed, every person working on this website must internalize what UmojaHub actually is.

UmojaHub is a verified agricultural marketplace and education platform built for East Africa. It operates two connected systems under one roof.

The first is the Food Security Hub. It is a marketplace where verified Kenyan farmers list produce, buyers purchase using M-Pesa mobile money, and cooperative groups of farmers collectively purchase agricultural inputs from verified suppliers. The platform does not take a commission or add markup. It provides the trust infrastructure — verification, ratings, price intelligence, and an AI farming assistant — that makes it possible for buyers to transact with confidence and farmers to earn fair prices without middlemen obscuring market rates.

The second is the Education Hub. It is a structured project verification platform for Kenyan computer science students. Students complete real software projects using either an AI-generated brief drawn from agricultural industry contexts or an open-source project they select. Their work passes through peer review and verified lecturer review. Successful projects are recorded in a permanent, verifiable portfolio. Employers can assess a student's portfolio knowing that every project in it passed a documented human review process.

These two hubs share a single platform, a single trust architecture, and a single verification philosophy: nothing claimed on UmojaHub is unverified.

## CORE CONCEPTS GLOSSARY

The following terms have specific meanings within UmojaHub. The website must use these terms consistently. Synonyms should not be introduced by copywriters or designers. Every term defined here must be explained on the website before it is used.

**Trust Score**: A numerical value between 0 and 100 assigned to every verified farmer. It reflects how much confidence the platform has accumulated in that farmer based on their verification status, transaction history, buyer ratings, and reliability record. It is calculated from four components and is recalculated automatically after relevant events.

**Trust Tier**: A named category assigned based on Trust Score ranges. The four tiers are NEW, ESTABLISHED, TRUSTED, and PREMIUM. Tiers are visible on listings and inform buyers about the depth of a farmer's track record on the platform.

**Verification**: The process by which UmojaHub confirms that a farmer, supplier, or lecturer is who they claim to be and meets the platform's requirements. For farmers, this involves document review by a platform administrator. For suppliers, it involves registration credential validation. For lecturers, it involves confirmation of academic standing. Verification is never self-asserted.

**Marketplace Listing**: A product record created by a verified farmer. A listing describes a specific crop or agricultural product, its quantity available, the price per unit, the harvest date, and the pickup or delivery location. Only verified farmers can create listings.

**Project Engagement**: A formal record of a student's work on a software project through the Education Hub. It contains the project brief, all submitted process documents, peer review results, and the final lecturer review decision. It is the unit of verification in the Education Hub.

**Project Brief**: The specification a student works from. On the AI_BRIEF track, it is generated by the platform's AI system using real agricultural industry contexts. On the OPEN_SOURCE track, it is derived from a real open-source repository the student selects.

**Peer Review**: A structured assessment of a student's submitted project documents by another student on the platform. Peer review is not optional and is not decorative. It is a required gate before a project reaches the lecturer review queue.

**Lecturer Review**: A structured four-dimension assessment of a student's project by a verified lecturer. The four dimensions are clarity, methodology, documentation quality, and reflection depth. The decision is one of three: VERIFIED, REVISION_REQUIRED, or DENIED.

**STK Push**: The mobile payment prompt that Safaricom (M-Pesa) sends to a buyer's phone when they place an order. The buyer receives a prompt on their phone and enters their M-Pesa PIN to authorize payment. UmojaHub never handles the buyer's payment credentials.

**Cooperative Group**: A collection of farmers on the platform who have organized themselves into a group for the purpose of placing collective bulk orders for agricultural inputs from verified suppliers.

**Verified Supplier**: An agricultural input supplier whose business registration numbers (including regulatory credentials such as KEBS, PCPB, and KEPHIS certification numbers) have been confirmed by a platform administrator and recorded in the platform's supplier directory.

**Price Intelligence**: A set of market data tools available to farmers showing their listed prices relative to verified weekly market benchmarks for ten major Kenyan crops, enabling farmers to make informed pricing decisions.

**Price Alert**: A threshold-based notification that a farmer configures. When a crop's market price crosses a specified threshold in either direction, the farmer receives an SMS notification.

**Portfolio**: A permanent, publicly accessible record of a student's verified project work. Every project in a portfolio has been reviewed by a verified lecturer and passed. The portfolio records the number of verified projects, skills demonstrated, and progression over time.

### AUDIENCE 1 — FARMERS

#### 1.1 Why Farmers Exist Within UmojaHub

Farmers are the supply side of the Food Security Hub. Without verified farmers listing produce, there is no marketplace. UmojaHub was built specifically to address the structural disadvantages that small and medium-scale Kenyan farmers face when trying to sell their produce: price opacity, lack of formal identity in commerce, no mechanism for building reputation with buyers they have never met, and dependence on intermediaries who obscure market prices.

The platform creates the conditions for direct commerce between farmer and buyer. For this to work, buyers must be able to trust farmers they have never met. The entire trust and verification architecture exists to make this possible.

#### 1.2 Problems Farmers Face That UmojaHub Addresses

**Price opacity**: Most farmers learn what produce is worth from the same traders who buy from them. This creates an inherent information asymmetry. Traders have access to market price data. Farmers typically do not. UmojaHub publishes weekly price benchmarks for major Kenyan crops derived from verified market sources, enabling farmers to see the difference between what middlemen offer and what the market actually supports.

**Trust deficit with unknown buyers**: When a farmer has no formal reputation, every buyer is a stranger who must decide whether to trust them. Without a verification and rating system, the decision to buy from an unknown farmer is based on nothing systematic. This means farmers who are honest and reliable cannot distinguish themselves from those who are not, and are priced accordingly.

**No formal identity in agricultural commerce**: Many Kenyan farmers operate without formal business registration. National identity documents and cooperative membership cards are their primary credentials. UmojaHub accepts these as the basis for verification, creating a formal platform identity for farmers who have never had one.

**Agronomic advice access**: Quality agronomic advice is expensive or geographically inaccessible for most small-scale farmers. Knowing what to plant, when to plant it, how to respond to weather conditions, and how to manage common crop problems requires access to expertise. The platform's AI Farm Assistant provides this access free of charge, with weather data integrated for the farmer's county.

**Agricultural input pricing**: Farmers buying inputs — seeds, fertilizer, pesticides — face the same information asymmetry they face when selling produce. Verified suppliers on the platform publish their input prices, and cooperative groups allow farmers to pool purchasing power for bulk rates.

**Price volatility with no warning**: Market prices for produce fluctuate weekly. A farmer who harvests at the wrong time or holds produce too long loses margin. Price alerts give farmers the ability to monitor market prices and receive SMS notification when prices cross their defined thresholds.

#### 1.3 Activities Available to Farmers

1. Create an account with name, email, phone number, and county location
2. Verify email address via a link sent to their registered email
3. Complete the farmer onboarding profile: crops grown, livestock kept, farm size in acres, and description
4. Submit verification documents: upload an image of their National ID, Cooperative Membership Card, or Passport to the platform
5. Await administrator review of submitted documents (administrators review the queue and approve or reject)
6. Receive SMS notification when a verification decision is made
7. If rejected, read the reason for rejection provided by the administrator and resubmit improved documentation
8. Once approved, create marketplace listings for specific crops
9. Set quantity available, price per kilogram or unit, harvest date, pickup or delivery location, and upload product images
10. Manage existing listings — update price, update quantity, mark as unavailable
11. Receive orders from buyers — view buyer contact information, order quantity, and total value
12. Monitor payment status for each order (the platform confirms when M-Pesa payment clears)
13. Receive SMS notification when a buyer's payment is confirmed
14. Begin order fulfillment after payment confirmation
15. Update order status to mark it as fulfilled
16. Receive SMS notification when a buyer marks an order as received
17. Receive ratings from buyers after order completion (1–5 scale)
18. Monitor their Trust Score and understand which component is influencing it
19. Set price alerts for specific crops at specific price thresholds
20. Receive SMS alerts when price thresholds are crossed
21. View the Price Intelligence dashboard to compare their listed prices against weekly market benchmarks for their crops
22. Create a cooperative group by naming it and setting a member limit
23. Invite other verified farmers to join their cooperative group
24. Join an existing cooperative group as a member
25. Propose a bulk input purchase order through their cooperative group, specifying the supplier and products needed
26. Use the AI Farm Assistant to ask questions about their crops, weather conditions, pests, soil health, and market timing
27. Access Knowledge Hub articles on agricultural topics curated by the platform
28. View their complete order history

#### 1.4 Complete Farmer Lifecycle on the Platform

**Stage 1 — Discovery and Registration**
A farmer discovers UmojaHub through word of mouth, a cooperative, an agricultural extension officer, or the platform's public marketplace. They can browse marketplace listings as a visitor. To sell, they register with their name, email, phone number, county, and a password meeting the platform's complexity requirements. They receive a verification email and must click the link before they can log in.

**Stage 2 — Profile Completion**
After email verification and first login, the farmer completes their profile: which crops they grow, what livestock they keep, their farm size in acres, and a description of their farm. This information appears on their public profile and informs the AI Farm Assistant's context.

**Stage 3 — Identity Verification**
To list produce, the farmer must pass identity verification. They upload an image of one of three accepted documents: their Kenyan National ID card, their Agricultural Cooperative membership card, or their Passport. The image must be clear, readable, and unobstructed. The platform stores the document URL and sets the farmer's verification status to PENDING.

**Stage 4 — Administrator Review**
A platform administrator reviews the verification queue. They examine the submitted document image, confirm the document type is valid, and make a decision: APPROVED or REJECTED. If rejected, they provide a written reason. The farmer receives an SMS notification regardless of the outcome.

**Stage 5 — Verification Approved**
Upon approval, the farmer's Trust Score is initialized with the verification component: 40 points. Their tier becomes ESTABLISHED. A verified badge appears on their profile and on any listings they create. They can now create marketplace listings.

**Stage 6 — First Listing**
The farmer creates their first marketplace listing. They specify: crop or product name, quantity available, price per unit, harvest date, pickup county and description, and at least one product image. The listing is published immediately and appears in the public marketplace.

**Stage 7 — First Order**
A buyer places an order on one of the farmer's listings. The platform atomically reserves the ordered quantity from the available inventory. The buyer initiates an M-Pesa STK Push payment. The farmer can see the pending order in their dashboard.

**Stage 8 — Payment Confirmation**
When Safaricom confirms the payment via their callback system, the platform updates the order status to PAID. The farmer receives an SMS: "Payment confirmed — [crop] order from [buyer name], KES [amount]. Order reference: UMJ-YYYY-XXXXXX."

**Stage 9 — Fulfillment**
The farmer prepares and delivers the order. They update the order status in their dashboard. The buyer receives a notification that their order is in fulfillment.

**Stage 10 — Order Received**
The buyer confirms receipt by updating the order status to RECEIVED. This triggers the platform to recalculate the farmer's Trust Score — the transaction component increases based on the order value. The buyer can now submit a rating.

**Stage 11 — Rating Received**
The buyer submits a 1–5 star rating. The farmer's rating component of their Trust Score updates. After a minimum of three ratings have been received, the rating component fully influences the Trust Score.

**Stage 12 — Trust Tier Progression**
As the farmer completes more orders, receives ratings, and maintains a reliable fulfillment record, their Trust Score climbs. At 60 points they enter the TRUSTED tier. At 80 points they enter the PREMIUM tier. Higher tiers increase buyer confidence and are displayed prominently on listings.

**Stage 13 — Cooperative Participation**
With experience on the platform, the farmer may form or join a cooperative group to purchase inputs collectively. Group orders reduce per-unit costs on seeds, fertilizer, and other inputs from verified suppliers.

**Stage 14 — Long-term Platform Presence**
An established farmer on the platform maintains an active inventory of listings, receives regular orders, monitors market prices through the Price Intelligence dashboard, and uses the AI Farm Assistant seasonally. Their Trust Score reflects their cumulative history on the platform.

#### 1.5 How Trust Works for Farmers

Trust on UmojaHub is not self-asserted. A farmer cannot claim to be trustworthy. Trust is accumulated through documented actions on the platform that are recorded, verified, and computed automatically.

The Trust Score has four components:

**Verification component (maximum 40 points)**: Awarded when a farmer's identity documents are reviewed and approved by a platform administrator. This is the foundation. A farmer cannot receive full trust without verified identity.

**Transaction component (maximum 25 points)**: Earned progressively through completed order volume. The more fulfilled orders a farmer accumulates, the higher this component climbs. It rewards sustained, consistent selling activity.

**Rating component (maximum 20 points)**: Calculated from buyer ratings across all completed orders. Requires a minimum of three ratings before the component fully activates. A farmer with fewer than three ratings receives partial credit. This prevents a single rating — positive or negative — from disproportionately affecting a farmer with limited history.

**Reliability component (maximum 15 points)**: Reflects a farmer's record of fulfilling orders without disputes being raised against them. Unfulfilled orders and disputed transactions reduce this component.

The Trust Score is recalculated automatically when: a verification decision is made, an order reaches RECEIVED status, a rating is submitted.

The Trust Tier is determined by the composite score:
- 0–39: NEW — the farmer has no verified history or very limited history
- 40–59: ESTABLISHED — the farmer has passed identity verification and has some transaction history
- 60–79: TRUSTED — the farmer has a substantial, consistent record of completed transactions with positive ratings
- 80–100: PREMIUM — the farmer has an extensive, high-quality transaction history with excellent ratings and no reliability issues

#### 1.6 What Farmers Are Responsible For

Farmers who use the platform accept specific responsibilities. These must be explained clearly on the website:

- Documents submitted for verification must be authentic. Submitting fraudulent documents results in permanent suspension.
- Listing quantities must be accurate. Listing produce that is not available, or at a quantity that cannot be fulfilled, harms the buyer and damages the farmer's reliability score.
- Prices must reflect the actual selling price. No bait-and-switch is permitted.
- Orders must be fulfilled after payment is confirmed. Abandoning a paid order is a serious violation.
- Product images must accurately represent the produce listed.

#### 1.7 Frequently Asked Questions — Farmers

**Can I sell on UmojaHub without being formally registered as a business?**
Yes. UmojaHub accepts personal identification documents — your National ID card, your Cooperative Membership Card, or your Passport. You do not need formal business registration to sell on the platform.

**How long does verification take?**
Verification is processed by a platform administrator. The typical review time is one to three business days, depending on the volume of pending applications. You will receive an SMS notification when a decision is made.

**What happens if my verification is rejected?**
You will receive an SMS and see the administrator's reason in your dashboard. Common reasons include: blurry document image, document expiry, or mismatch between the name on the document and the name on your account. You can correct the issue and resubmit. There is no penalty for resubmission.

**Can I list produce before I am verified?**
No. Identity verification is required before you can create any listing. The platform only shows verified farmers' produce to buyers.

**What if a buyer does not pay after placing an order?**
When a buyer places an order, the payment process begins immediately. The platform initiates an M-Pesa STK Push to the buyer's phone. The buyer must authorize payment on their phone within minutes. If payment is not completed, the order is automatically cancelled and your inventory is restored. Your Trust Score is not affected by a buyer's failure to complete payment.

**What if a buyer claims they did not receive their order when they did?**
Buyers can mark an order as received through the platform. Disputes are reviewed by platform administrators. Maintain records of your delivery — photographs and messages — in case a dispute arises.

**How does the AI Farm Assistant work? Can it give me wrong advice?**
The Farm Assistant is powered by an AI language model with access to your farm profile and current weather data for your county. It provides general agronomic guidance. It is not a replacement for a certified agricultural extension officer or veterinarian. For significant decisions involving crop treatment, chemical application, or animal health, consult a qualified professional. The Farm Assistant is a starting point for information, not a final authority.

**Is my phone number visible to buyers?**
Your contact information is shared with buyers only after they place an order. Before an order, buyers see your listings, your Trust Score, your tier, and your verified status — not your personal contact details.

**What fees does UmojaHub charge?**
The platform does not charge listing fees or commissions on sales. The M-Pesa transaction incurs standard Safaricom transfer fees, which are determined by Safaricom's pricing and are charged to the buyer.

**Can I be on UmojaHub and also sell through middlemen?**
Yes. UmojaHub does not require exclusivity. You can sell through the platform and through any other channel simultaneously.

**What crops can I list?**
Any agricultural produce grown in Kenya. There is no restricted list. Common crops listed include maize, beans, tomatoes, potatoes, kale, onions, and fruit. The Price Intelligence dashboard provides benchmarks for ten major crops; this does not limit what you can list.

#### 1.8 Common Misconceptions — Farmers

**Misconception: UmojaHub is another mobile money service.**
UmojaHub uses M-Pesa for payment processing but is not affiliated with Safaricom. It is an agricultural marketplace. M-Pesa is the payment method, not the product.

**Misconception: Verified farmers get a guaranteed number of buyers.**
Verification enables you to list. It does not guarantee sales. Building a buyer base requires responsive fulfillment, accurate listings, and a growing positive rating history.

**Misconception: The Trust Score is based on how long you have been on the platform.**
Trust is based on actions, not time. A farmer who completes ten orders in two months builds trust faster than a farmer who completes three orders in a year.

**Misconception: If I am rejected for verification, my account is terminated.**
Rejection means your document submission was not accepted. Your account remains active. You can correct the issue and resubmit.

#### 1.9 Farmer First-Visit Navigation Path

A farmer visiting the website for the first time should be directed: For Farmers page → How Verification Works → Trust & Verification methodology → Price Intelligence explanation → Register.

### AUDIENCE 2 — BUYERS

#### 2.1 Why Buyers Exist Within UmojaHub

Buyers are the demand side of the Food Security Hub. They are individuals, households, restaurants, hotels, schools, hospitals, food processors, and any other entity that purchases fresh agricultural produce. Without buyers, the marketplace has no commercial function. UmojaHub enables buyers to purchase verified produce directly from verified farmers, eliminating the layers of intermediaries that add cost and reduce freshness without adding accountability.

#### 2.2 Problems Buyers Face

**No way to verify farmer legitimacy**: Before UmojaHub, a buyer purchasing from an unknown farmer had no mechanism to assess whether that farmer was credible, had fulfilled orders for others, or had a history of accurate listings.

**Price opacity from the other side**: Buyers also lack transparency into whether they are paying a fair market price. Middlemen markup produce without disclosing the farm-gate price.

**No recourse when produce quality does not match listing**: A buyer who receives produce that does not match the listed description has no formal platform to report this, no rating mechanism to warn other buyers, and no way to affect the seller's future business.

**No aggregated marketplace**: Before UmojaHub, finding produce directly from farmers required personal networks or physical market visits. There was no single searchable directory of available produce from verified sources.

#### 2.3 Activities Available to Buyers

1. Browse the public marketplace without an account
2. Filter listings by crop type, county, price range, and farmer trust tier
3. Search for specific crops by name
4. View listing detail: crop name, description, quantity available, price, harvest date, pickup location, farmer's Trust Score, farmer's tier, and verified badge status
5. Register for an account to place orders
6. Verify email address
7. Place an order by specifying the quantity desired
8. Complete payment via M-Pesa STK Push on their phone
9. Monitor payment status while awaiting Safaricom's confirmation
10. Receive notification when payment is confirmed
11. Track fulfillment status as the farmer processes the order
12. Mark an order as received upon delivery
13. Submit a rating (1–5 stars) for the farmer after receiving the order
14. View complete order history
15. Cancel a pending order before payment confirmation

#### 2.4 Complete Buyer Lifecycle on the Platform

**Stage 1 — Discovery**
A buyer discovers the marketplace through any channel. They can browse all listings, view prices, and see farmer Trust Scores without creating an account. The marketplace is publicly accessible.

**Stage 2 — Registration Decision**
When the buyer decides to place an order, they must register. Registration requires: name, email, phone number, and password. Email verification is required before the account is active.

**Stage 3 — Finding Produce**
The buyer uses the marketplace's search and filter tools to find the produce they need. They can filter by crop, county, price range, and minimum trust tier. They can view individual listing details and the farmer's complete public profile including Trust Score and verified badge.

**Stage 4 — Placing an Order**
The buyer selects a listing and specifies the quantity they want. The platform confirms the quantity is available and presents the total price. The buyer confirms the order.

**Stage 5 — M-Pesa Payment**
The platform sends an STK Push to the buyer's registered phone number. The buyer receives a prompt on their phone, enters their M-Pesa PIN, and authorizes the payment. The entire payment is processed by Safaricom's system. UmojaHub does not handle the buyer's payment credentials at any point.

**Stage 6 — Payment Confirmation**
When Safaricom confirms the payment, both the farmer and buyer receive SMS notifications. The order status updates to PAID and IN_FULFILLMENT.

**Stage 7 — Fulfillment**
The farmer prepares and delivers the order. The buyer can view the fulfillment status in their order history.

**Stage 8 — Receipt Confirmation**
When the buyer receives their order, they mark it as received in the platform. This triggers the platform to update the farmer's Trust Score.

**Stage 9 — Rating**
The buyer submits a rating for the farmer. Ratings are specific to this order and cannot be submitted more than once per order. Ratings permanently influence the farmer's Trust Score.

#### 2.5 How Trust Works for Buyers

Buyers benefit from trust but are not themselves the subject of it. The trust system is designed to give buyers the confidence to transact with farmers they have never met.

When a buyer views a listing, they see:
- Whether the farmer is verified (documented identity confirmed by admin)
- The farmer's Trust Score (0–100)
- The farmer's Trust Tier (NEW/ESTABLISHED/TRUSTED/PREMIUM)
- The number of completed transactions in the farmer's history
- The farmer's average rating from past buyers

This information is enough to make an informed decision. A buyer choosing between two farmers can see which one has a longer track record, better ratings, and higher reliability.

Buyers who submit ratings contribute directly to the trust system. An accurate, honest rating helps future buyers make better decisions and rewards farmers who perform well.

#### 2.6 Frequently Asked Questions — Buyers

**Can I browse produce without an account?**
Yes. The entire marketplace is publicly browsable without registration. You only need to create an account when you want to place an order.

**Is M-Pesa the only payment method?**
Currently yes. UmojaHub is built for the Kenyan market where M-Pesa is the dominant mobile money platform. Your M-Pesa-registered phone number must match the phone number on your UmojaHub account.

**What if my M-Pesa payment goes through but my order is not confirmed?**
The platform detects payment confirmation from Safaricom's callback system. If a confirmed payment is not reflected in your order status within 15 minutes, contact platform support. Your payment is recorded and can be reconciled.

**Can I cancel an order?**
You can cancel a pending order before payment is confirmed. Once payment is confirmed, the order is in fulfillment and cannot be cancelled through the platform. Contact support to discuss your situation.

**What if the produce I receive does not match the listing?**
Submit your rating and describe the discrepancy. Platform support can review your report. Significant misrepresentation affects the farmer's Trust Score and may result in listing suspension or account review.

**Are all farmers on the platform verified?**
All farmers who have listings on the platform have passed identity verification. Only verified farmers can create listings. A farmer who has not completed verification cannot list produce.

**What does a farmer's tier tell me?**
NEW means the farmer has limited or no transaction history on the platform. ESTABLISHED means they have passed verification and have some transaction history. TRUSTED means they have a substantial history of completed orders and positive ratings. PREMIUM means they have an extensive history with excellent ratings and no reliability issues. Tier is a summary of trust accumulation, not a guarantee.

**Is it safe to share my M-Pesa PIN on this platform?**
UmojaHub does not ask for your M-Pesa PIN at any point. When you place an order, Safaricom sends a payment prompt directly to your phone. You enter your PIN into your phone, not into any UmojaHub interface. This is the standard, secure M-Pesa STK Push mechanism.

### AUDIENCE 3 — SUPPLIERS

#### 3.1 Why Suppliers Exist Within UmojaHub

Verified Suppliers are agricultural input businesses — sellers of seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, farming tools, and related products. They serve the cooperative purchasing side of the Food Security Hub. When cooperative groups of farmers want to bulk-purchase agricultural inputs, they place group orders through the platform from verified suppliers. Suppliers are not self-registered. They are added to the platform's supplier directory by platform administrators after their regulatory credentials are verified.

#### 3.2 What Suppliers Enable

Suppliers make the input side of farming more transparent and accessible. Without verified suppliers in the directory, cooperative groups have no formal purchasing mechanism through the platform. With verified suppliers, farmers can access confirmed, credentialed businesses rather than navigating a market where supplier legitimacy is difficult to establish.

#### 3.3 Supplier Verification

Suppliers in the UmojaHub directory have had the following confirmed by a platform administrator:
- Business registration credentials
- Relevant regulatory certification numbers: KEBS (Kenya Bureau of Standards), PCPB (Pest Control Products Board), and/or KEPHIS (Kenya Plant Health Inspectorate Service) numbers where applicable
- County of operation
- Category of inputs supplied (seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, tools, etc.)

These credentials appear in the supplier's directory listing. Buyers and farmers can see exactly which regulatory bodies have certified the supplier and cross-reference independently.

#### 3.4 How Suppliers Interact with the Platform

Suppliers do not have self-service accounts on the current platform version. Their directory listings are managed by administrators. When a cooperative group places a bulk order specifying a supplier, the supplier is notified through contact information held by the administrator. The group ordering process coordinates between the platform and the supplier outside the platform's payment system in the current implementation.

#### 3.5 What the Supplier Directory Page Must Explain

The public supplier directory explains:
- What a Verified Supplier designation means
- Which regulatory credentials are checked and why
- How to have a supplier added to the directory (contact platform administrators)
- The difference between a listed supplier and an unlisted supplier
- What information appears for each supplier

### AUDIENCE 4 — STUDENTS

#### 4.1 Why Students Exist Within UmojaHub

Students — specifically Kenyan computer science students — face a documented problem in East Africa's technology hiring market: academic qualifications are difficult for employers to use as a proxy for practical skill. A degree says what topics a student studied. It does not say what they built, how they documented their work, or whether another qualified person reviewed it.

The Education Hub exists to create a portable, verifiable record of actual project work. It is not a job board. It is not a bootcamp. It is a structured documentation and review system that produces a permanent, publicly accessible portfolio where every project entry has been peer-reviewed and lecturer-reviewed.

#### 4.2 Problems Students Face

**Credential gap**: Employers cannot distinguish a student who completed their coursework genuinely from one who did not. Grades are an institutional output, not a portfolio of evidence.

**Portfolio inauthenticity risk**: Self-reported portfolios (GitHub profiles, personal websites) cannot be verified. An employer looking at a GitHub repository cannot confirm who wrote the code, whether the student understood it, or whether the documentation was theirs.

**Lack of structured project guidance**: Students working on independent projects often lack structured briefs that reflect real industry requirements. Projects chosen without guidance tend to be either too simple or poorly defined.

**No structured feedback**: Most students complete independent projects without any review from a qualified professional. Mistakes in methodology, documentation, and problem-framing go uncorrected.

**Access to industry contexts**: Particularly for students from non-urban areas, industry project contexts — what companies actually need built, what problems they face — are not easily accessible.

#### 4.3 Activities Available to Students

1. Register as a student with name, email, phone number, and academic institution
2. Verify email address
3. Complete student onboarding
4. Choose a project track: AI_BRIEF (platform generates a brief from agricultural industry contexts) or OPEN_SOURCE (student provides a GitHub repository URL and receives a brief based on a selected open-source project)
5. Receive the AI-generated project brief specifying: the problem to solve, the deliverables required, the target users, and the evaluation criteria
6. Use the AI Mentor to ask questions about their brief, their approach, their technical choices, and their documentation
7. Submit the Breakdown Document: an analysis of the problem, the chosen approach, and the technologies to be used
8. Submit the Plan Document: a structured implementation plan with milestones and timeline
9. Submit the Reflection Document: a honest assessment of what was completed, what was not, what was learned, and what would be done differently
10. Submit the project for peer review (requires all three documents to be submitted first)
11. Receive a peer reviewer assignment — another student on the platform reviews their work
12. Complete a peer review for another student's submission
13. Advance to lecturer review queue after peer review
14. Await review from a verified lecturer
15. Receive the lecturer's decision: VERIFIED, REVISION_REQUIRED, or DENIED
16. If REVISION_REQUIRED, address the feedback and resubmit
17. View their verified portfolio
18. Share their portfolio URL with employers and institutions
19. Track their portfolio progression over multiple projects

#### 4.4 Complete Student Lifecycle on the Platform

**Stage 1 — Registration and Onboarding**
The student registers with their institution affiliation. They verify their email. During onboarding they complete their student profile.

**Stage 2 — Track Selection**
At the beginning of each project, the student selects one of two tracks. On the AI_BRIEF track, the platform generates a brief by selecting from its curated library of agricultural industry project contexts, filtered by complexity tier. The student cannot select the specific context — the platform assigns one. This prevents cherry-picking easy topics. On the OPEN_SOURCE track, the student provides a GitHub repository URL. The platform analyzes the repository and generates a brief based on the project's actual needs and open issues.

**Stage 3 — Active Development**
The student works on their project. They have access to the AI Mentor for guidance. The Mentor uses a Socratic approach — it asks questions rather than giving answers, pushing the student to reason through problems independently. The Mentor has context about the student's brief and their current stage.

The student must produce three documents to advance:

*Breakdown Document*: Demonstrates that the student understood the brief. Must show: decomposition of the problem into components, analysis of the chosen technical approach, explanation of why this approach was chosen over alternatives, and identification of technical risks.

*Plan Document*: Demonstrates that the student can plan structured work. Must show: implementation milestones, task decomposition, timeline, and resource requirements.

*Reflection Document*: Demonstrates honest self-assessment. Must show: what was completed, what was not completed and why, what was learned technically, what was learned about process, and what would be done differently. The Reflection Document is considered the most significant indicator of professional maturity by the review system.

**Stage 4 — Submission**
When all three documents are submitted, the student submits the project for review. This is a one-way action — it cannot be undone. The submission is timestamped and locked.

**Stage 5 — Peer Review**
The platform assigns the student as a peer reviewer for another student's submission. The student must complete their assigned peer review before proceeding. This is mandatory — it cannot be bypassed. The peer review is a structured assessment that requires the reviewer to evaluate the submission against the same criteria a lecturer will use.

**Stage 6 — Lecturer Review Queue**
After peer review is complete, the project enters the lecturer review queue. Verified lecturers select projects to review from the queue. The student waits for a decision. This may take several days depending on queue length.

**Stage 7 — Lecturer Decision**
The lecturer reviews all three documents, the student's brief, and the peer review. They assess four dimensions: clarity, methodology, documentation quality, and reflection depth. Each dimension requires at least 50 words of substantive comment. The decision is one of three:

VERIFIED: The project meets the standard. It is permanently recorded in the student's portfolio.
REVISION_REQUIRED: The project has identified weaknesses. The student must address specific feedback and resubmit.
DENIED: The project does not meet the standard and cannot be resubmitted. The student may start a new project engagement.

**Stage 8 — Portfolio Publication**
A VERIFIED decision permanently adds the project to the student's portfolio. The portfolio records: the project name, the track, the industry context (where applicable), the completion date, the skills demonstrated, and the verification status. The portfolio is publicly accessible via a URL.

#### 4.5 How Trust and Verification Work for Students

Student verification is project-level, not identity-level. Each project in a portfolio carries the weight of its own verification: a peer review and a lecturer review. The process is documented in a verification audit log that records: the document hashes at the time of submission (ensuring documents cannot be altered after submission), the peer review outcome, the lecturer's scores on each dimension, and the final decision.

A student cannot misrepresent a project in their portfolio because the verification record is independent of what the student claims. An employer can see exactly: when the project was submitted, what the decision was, and on what criteria it was evaluated.

#### 4.6 Frequently Asked Questions — Students

**Can I submit the same project more than once?**
No. Each project engagement is for a unique project. You cannot reuse a previous submission.

**Can I see who my peer reviewer is?**
The peer review process is structured but not anonymous — the reviewer's identity is visible to the submission author after the review is complete.

**What if my project is DENIED?**
A DENIED decision means the project did not meet the minimum standard for the platform and cannot be resubmitted in its current form. You can begin a new project engagement with a new brief. A DENIED decision is not recorded visibly in your public portfolio — only VERIFIED projects appear.

**What if I disagree with my lecturer's decision?**
The platform does not currently have a formal appeals process. A lecturer's REVISION_REQUIRED feedback contains specific commentary on what needs to be addressed. A DENIED decision is final. The quality of the review is monitored through the platform's lecturer effectiveness tracking.

**Who are the lecturers reviewing my work?**
All lecturers on the platform have been individually verified by a platform administrator who confirmed their academic standing. Their verification status is displayed in their profile.

**What happens to my documents after submission?**
Your documents are stored permanently. Their content is hashed at the time of submission to create a tamper-evident record. This means the content cannot be changed after submission and the hash recorded in the audit log permanently represents what you submitted.

**Can my employer independently verify my portfolio?**
Yes. Portfolio entries contain enough information — including the verification date, the brief type, and the reviewer decision — for an employer to contact the platform for verification if needed. The public portfolio URL is shareable and permanently accessible.

**Does the AI Mentor write my documents for me?**
No. The AI Mentor is designed to ask questions, not to produce answers. It will not generate your breakdown, plan, or reflection for you. It will ask you questions about your thinking, push back on weak reasoning, and point you toward resources. Using the AI Mentor to generate your documents would be a misuse of the system and would produce documents that would not survive reviewer scrutiny.

#### 5.2 Activities Available to Lecturers

8. Write minimum 50-word substantive commentary on each dimension
9. Make a decision: VERIFIED, REVISION_REQUIRED, or DENIED
10. Submit the review
11. View their own effectiveness statistics: number of reviews completed, average scores given, and decision distribution

- Academic or professional credentials
- Teaching or industry experience in a relevant field
- Identity

#### 5.4 Frequently Asked Questions — Lecturers

A thorough review of three documents — typically totaling 2,000–5,000 words of student writing — with substantive commentary on four dimensions takes approximately 30–60 minutes for a careful reviewer.

**Can I review projects from my own institution's students?**
The platform does not currently enforce institutional conflict-of-interest restrictions in the review assignment system. Lecturers are expected to exercise professional judgment and recuse themselves from reviewing students they know personally.

**Is there compensation for reviews?**
The current platform version does not offer monetary compensation for lecturer reviews. Lecturers participate as professional contributors to a public good.

## PART FOUR — CONTENT STRATEGY

### Long-form Educational Content

The following long-form content pieces must exist as standalone pages or dedicated sections:

**A Farmer's Guide to Trust Scores**: A complete explanation of how a farmer can understand, monitor, and improve their Trust Score. Explains what each component requires. Gives realistic timelines for tier progression. Does not promise outcomes. Written for a farmer who has just passed verification and wants to understand what comes next.

**Understanding M-Pesa Payments on UmojaHub**: A complete explanation of the M-Pesa payment process from the perspective of both buyer and farmer. Explains what STK Push is, what the buyer experiences, what the farmer experiences, and what happens in each failure scenario. Specifically explains that UmojaHub never handles the buyer's PIN or payment credentials.

**The Education Hub Review Process — A Complete Guide**: Explains the three documents, peer review, and lecturer review in enough depth that a student who reads it knows exactly what will be evaluated and what standard they must meet. Includes examples of what substantive reflection looks like versus superficial reflection.

**How Verification Works — Methodology Document**: A public-facing methodology document explaining the platform's approach to verification for each actor type. Different from the Trust & Verification page — this is a longer, more detailed treatment.

**Price Intelligence Explained**: A guide to the Price Intelligence dashboard for farmers. Explains what benchmarks are, where they come from, how to read the comparison between a farmer's listed price and the market benchmark, and what a platform premium percentage means.

**Cooperative Ordering Guide**: A complete guide to forming and managing cooperative groups. What the process involves, how bulk ordering works, what the minimum requirements are, and how members join and contribute.

**Verification Decision Statistics**: The overall approval and rejection rate for farmer verifications, without exposing individual records. Demonstrates that verification is real and not automatic.

**Lecturer Effectiveness Summary**: Aggregate statistics on lecturer review activity — number of reviews completed, average decision distribution. Shows the review system is active.

## PART FIVE — VISUAL STRATEGY

This section specifies what types of visuals belong on each major page and what they communicate. It does not specify visual design, style, or aesthetics.

### Visual Principles

Every visual on the website must justify its existence by answering: what does the visitor understand after seeing this that they did not understand before? Decorative visuals are prohibited. Every image, diagram, photograph, and screenshot must communicate specific information.

### Visual Type Definitions

**Process diagram**: A sequential illustration showing a workflow, with each step labeled. Arrows indicate direction of flow. Each node names an actor and an action. Used when explaining workflows, payment flows, and lifecycle stages.

**Component diagram**: A visual showing the parts of a system and their relationships. Used for the Trust Score components, the three-document structure, and the review process.

**Annotated screenshot**: A real screenshot of the platform with labeled callouts pointing to specific interface elements. Used to show buyers what a listing looks like, students what the review feedback looks like, and farmers what their Trust Score display shows.

**Data visualization**: A chart, graph, or number display showing real platform metrics. Used on the Transparency page and Homepage statistics section.

**Educational infographic**: A structured visual explaining a concept that is difficult to convey in text alone. Used for the Trust Tier progression and the M-Pesa payment flow.

**Map**: A geographic visualization. Used to show county coverage — which counties have verified farmers on the platform.

**Photography**: Photographs of actual farmers, buyers, students, or agricultural contexts. Used only on the About page and potentially the For Farmers page. Must be genuine photography of real people, not stock images. If genuine photography is not available, this asset type should not be used.

### Visual Assignments by Page

**Homepage**: Process diagrams (marketplace flow, education flow), platform statistics data display, trust system overview diagram.

**For Farmers**: Verification flow diagram, Trust Score component diagram, Trust Tier ladder, M-Pesa payment flow diagram, Price Intelligence annotated screenshot.

**For Buyers**: Marketplace listing anatomy annotated screenshot, M-Pesa payment flow diagram, Trust Tier explanation visual.

**For Students**: Education Hub workflow diagram, three-document structure diagram, peer + lecturer review relationship diagram, portfolio entry anatomy annotated screenshot.

**For Lecturers**: Review process flow diagram, four-dimension assessment visual.

**Trust & Verification**: Verification process diagrams for each actor type, Trust Score component breakdown, audit trail explanation diagram.

**Marketplace**: Annotated listing view showing where trust information appears.

**Transparency**: Platform activity data visualizations, county coverage map.

## PART SIX — ANIMATION STRATEGY

Animation must only be used when motion communicates something that static visuals cannot. It must never be used for decoration.

### Sections That Justify Animation

**Trust Score component breakdown on the For Farmers page**: The component bars could animate to their values on scroll entry. This communicates that the score is calculated from discrete, measurable components — the motion shows construction, not decoration.

**Trust Tier progression on the For Farmers and Trust & Verification pages**: A progression animation showing a score increasing through tiers over time. Communicates that trust is earned progressively, not assigned.

**M-Pesa payment flow on the For Buyers and For Farmers pages**: A sequential step-by-step animation showing the STK Push flow — each step activating in sequence. Communicates the real-time, sequential nature of the payment process better than a static diagram.

**Education Hub workflow on the For Students page**: The pipeline from brief generation through portfolio publication could animate with each stage activating sequentially. Communicates the ordered, linear nature of the workflow.

**Homepage marketplace flow**: A simplified animation of the three-step marketplace flow — list, pay, fulfill — activated on scroll.

### Sections That Must Remain Static

**Platform statistics**: Numbers should not animate (count-up animations are a conversion pattern, not an information pattern). Display final values immediately.

**Trust Score methodology**: The explanation requires reading, not watching. Animation would distract from comprehension.

**Verification process details**: Detailed process documentation must be static for readability.

**Legal pages**: Entirely static.

**Help Center**: Entirely static.

**All FAQ sections**: Static.

### Animation Rules

- All animations must be skippable or reducible (respect prefers-reduced-motion)
- Animations must not begin until the section is in the viewport
- Animations must not loop — they communicate state, not energy
- Duration: 300–800ms for transitions, no longer
- Easing: Ease-in-out only — no bounce, no spring, no elastic effects

## PART EIGHT — CONTENT PROHIBITED ON THE WEBSITE

The following content is explicitly prohibited. Any copy, visual, or element that falls into these categories must be rejected in review:

- Taglines or slogans
- Emotional or aspirational language ("transforming agriculture," "empowering farmers")
- Unverified statistics (market size, industry projections, employment outcome claims)
- Testimonials from users (real or fabricated)
- Social proof elements (unless they are factual platform statistics)
- Claims about competitors or the status quo that are not factually sourced
- Promise-based language about what the platform will do for the user
- Stock photography
- Icons used as decorative elements rather than informational ones
- Color-coding used for aesthetic rather than communicative purpose
- Any section that exists to build excitement rather than understanding

## PART NINE — CONTENT REVIEW CRITERIA

Before any content block is approved for the website, it must pass the following questions:

1. Does this content make the visitor more informed, or does it make them more excited?
2. Does every claim made in this content have a factual basis in the platform's actual capabilities?
3. Can a visitor act on this information without additional clarification?
4. Does this content leave the visitor with unanswered questions that this page should have answered?
5. Would removing this content leave the visitor less informed? (If no: remove it)
6. Does this content expose any implementation detail that should remain internal? (If yes: revise)
7. Does this content create an expectation the platform cannot reliably fulfill? (If yes: revise)
